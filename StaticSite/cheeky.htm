The Perfect Omelette

With care anyone can master the art of omelette making.
Delicate handling is required, but practice makes perfect.

Have everything ready before cooking your omelette, including a hot plate upon which to serve it.
An omelette must never wait, but should be waited for.

A few minutes before cooking, place your pan on a low heat to ensure that it is heated evenly. Add a little of the fat to the pan before heating if the manufacturer advises.

This recipe serves one.

Equipment

  • An omelette pan. Alternatively a thick cast iron, copper or aluminium pan, 15-18cm in diameter.
  • A spatula.
  • A whisk.
  • A palette knife.

Ingredients

  • Butter for greasing the pan. Bacon fat may be used instead.
  • 2 Eggs
  • 15ml Milk
  • Salt and Pepper

Method

  • Break the eggs into a bowl and whisk together. Do not make them frothy, as overbeating spoils the texture of the finished omelette.
  • Season with salt and pepper and stir in the milk.
  • Add a knob of butter to the pan to grease it slightly. The pan should be good and hot, but still on a low heat.
  • Pour the beaten eggs into the hot fat.
  • Stir gently with the back of a wooden spatula, drawing the mixture from the sides to the centre. As it sets, let the liquid egg from the centre run to the sides of the pan.
  • When the egg has set, stop stirring and cook for another minute until it is golden underneath and still creamy on top.
  • If you are adding a filling, this is the time to do so. Almost anything is suitable, but good choices are fines herbes, cheese, tomato, mushrooms or bacon.
  • Tilt the pan away from you and use the palette knife to turn over a third of the omelette into the centre. Repeat for the opposite third.
  • Turn out onto your warmed plate with the folded sides underneath. Don't overcook or the omelette will be tough.
